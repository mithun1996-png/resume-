About Me

I am a doctoral researcher in exoplanet atmospheric science at the School of Earth and Planetary Sciences, NISER, India. My research lies at the intersection of astrophysics, atmospheric physics, and data-driven modeling, with a strong focus on the characterization of exoplanet atmospheres using both reflected-light and thermal emission observations.

My primary scientific interests include reflected-light spectroscopy, direct imaging of Earth-like exoplanets, and the development of physically consistent atmospheric retrieval frameworks. I work on forward modeling of planetary spectra incorporating radiative transfer, cloud and haze microphysics, and surface reflectance.

A significant component of my work involves Bayesian atmospheric retrievals and statistical inference. My research is motivated by observations from JWST and future missions such as the Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO) and LIFE, particularly for biosignature detectability and habitability assessment.

Alongside research, I am committed to science education and outreach. I am the founder of Q-Physics Academy, mentoring students and developing advanced physics and astronomy content.
